# Editorial and visual contract

Write sharply funny, specific league journalism. Earn jokes with a team's actual score, choice, player or history. Use a neutral narrator: no Boston, Patriots, manager, or fantasy-team allegiance. Use coherent paragraphs and complete sentences with natural variation. The old average-sentence-length limit is superseded. Avoid repetitive slogans and empty hype; retain dry wit and decisive takes. Historical team labels are observations, not license to invent history.

Keep this reading order: masthead; lead with three big stats and 3–4 paragraphs; all 12 power rankings in exact Elo order; Elo progression chart; all six matchup recaps; four awards in a desktop 2×2 grid; full-width position standouts; aligned injuries; transactions with waiver table, trades, free agents and drop-only moves; all six next-week pairings. A fifth award is optional when supported, not filler.

Use the regular newspaper template `templates/card-back.html`. Each match report spans the full width with a compact scoreboard on the left, headline/recap on the right, and player details below grouped by fantasy team. Stack on phones. Preserve every team, score and player detail. Within match reports, omit manager metadata, WINNER/LOST badges, separate margin badges, source credits, methods, source links and AI-credit footer. A margin belongs in natural prose when useful. Schema 3 still carries match-report manager fields privately for verification and backward compatibility; the match-report template omits them. Rankings retain the existing team/record metadata.

Waiver and standout tables span the content width. Transactions include successful claims, zero-dollar claims, free agents and drop-only moves. Failed bids stay separate from completed moves and FAAB spending. Describe trades including picks and FAAB transfers. Avoid repeated empty commas, duplicate DEF labels, and placeholder 'No addition' text. Use a player’s actual NFL stat line, not a fantasy total alone, to explain performances. Reconcile player points to this league’s scoring.

Check every numerical claim in context, all lineup choices, roster ownership as of cutoff, and the dates of injuries and news. Describe unknowns precisely in the private evidence. Never treat today’s injury flag as last Wednesday’s status. Preview schedules may be future; preview results may not. Tie games use the legacy private winner/loser side fields with equal scores; the jump list renders both sides as Tied. Report the tie explicitly in the recap and avoid inventing a winner.

For template changes, inspect phone and desktop readability, team jumps, tables and chart. Ordinary weekly writing needs a readable page and sound facts, not review paperwork.

All league managers are male (AJ, October 28, 2026). Use he/him/his for an individual manager. Retain grammatically valid plural they/their for multiple people or teams; distinguish a team reference from its individual manager. Do not mechanically rewrite quoted facts or archived approved editions.

Do not render the bench/nonstarter commentary block beneath Standouts. Keep the table and all rows, then continue to Injuries. Other two-column sections remain. Keep underlying player and nonstarter data available for writing.
